Setup & Operation

Cue Performance

Frame rate for every cue, measured on the show hardware, with the counts that explain it. The short version: cost tracks VFX instances, not particles.

✅

Current build: v1.14.0 (commit 9fa22e1, branch director-mode), 23 August 2026. This is the build the soak results and the before/after comparison below were measured on. It contains Nina’s VFX optimisation work, the stem-name fix that restored audio to B_SQ601.5, and director mode.

⚠️ The per-cue table further down is older — it was measured on v1.7.0 (commit 611abf8) on 21 August and has not been re-measured since. Instance counts in particular have changed: Nina’s work took the heaviest cues from 32 VFX instances to 28. Treat that table as the shape of the problem, not as current numbers.

Hardware throughout: 21 August 2026. Beam Pro X4000 driving XREAL One Pro glasses in stereo, and a Galaxy Tab S9 (SM-X710) in mono. Both read audio from psychojelly/opera-audio. Figures come from the build’s own PerfProbe, which waits 2.5 seconds after each cue so the content has actually spawned.

Performance · Findings

What the numbers say

Three tiers, and they line up almost exactly with the VFX instance count.

InstancesTypical frame rateWhere
456–60 fpsSQ301–SQ601 opening material
2029–30 fpsthe SQ7xx “How Long” chord sequence
29–3314–32 fpscommunion, shadow and the endings

Particle count barely correlates. B_VQ801 runs at 14 fps with 40,715 particles, while cues at twice that frame rate carry 54,000. What separates them is instances: 32 against 20. Cost is instances multiplied by output contexts, and that is the lever worth pulling.

⚠️

These are best-case figures. Taken on a freshly launched, cool device. The same cues measured more than twice as slow during a sustained run once the Beam Pro had warmed up — B_VQ503.x read about 30 ms here and 77 ms after 25 minutes of load, at 44–47 °C. A rehearsal produces the second set of numbers, not the first.

The tablet is not the constraint. Every cue holds 30 fps on the Tab S9, which renders mono at full resolution while the Beam Pro renders the scene twice for stereo on phone-class hardware. Everything below is a Beam Pro limit.

Performance · Before and after

Did Nina’s optimisation make it faster?

Mixed. The counts came down as intended and most cues are unchanged or better — but the three heaviest cues in the show got substantially slower.

Measured as two full 50-cue passes on the same Beam Pro, same cue order, 15 second spacing. Both open at 17 ms and follow the same thermal curve, so they are like for like.

The optimisation itself worked. The heaviest cues dropped from 32 VFX instances to 28 and from 50 renderers to 48, and total particles fell by about 6%. That is real, and it is the lever this page has always said matters.

But the endings regressed sharply.

⚠️

B_VQ503.11 is the one to look at. It got 2.7× slower while using fewer instances (32→28) and fewer particles (52,579→37,346). Every counter this page tracks went down and the cost went up, which means the expense moved somewhere the counters cannot see — almost certainly per-pixel shading rather than instance count.

That is the same signature as the Lit/Unlit regression: particle and instance counts barely moved while frame time went 3–19× worse. Worth checking whether the new crown work reintroduced per-pixel cost of that kind.

Net effect on the show: the median is flat, so a rehearsal will feel about the same for most of the run — but the ending, already the most expensive stretch, is now materially worse.

Performance · Soak

Sustained runs on v1.14.0

Every trial opens at 17 ms (~59 fps) and closes around 34–38 ms. That degradation is reproducible across runs, so it can be planned around rather than treated as noise. The heavy cues are the same three every time: B_VQ801, B_SQ901, B_VQ901.

⚠️

Distinguish a crash from a disconnect before drawing conclusions. On 23 August the glasses disconnected six times, each time killing the app. That is more lost test time than the crash caused. Android records the difference: reason=5 APP CRASH (NATIVE) status=11 is a real SIGSEGV; reason=2 SIGNALED status=9 is a SIGKILL from session loss. Read it with:

adb shell dumpsys activity exit-info com.joanofthecity.xreal

Inferring the cause from logs alone produced three wrong calls in one day. Ask Android instead.

1. The wings set the cost of the entire 7xx block

The “How Long” chord sequence is 23 cues — the longest continuous stretch in the show. Walked cue by cue, it is completely flat: everything from B_VQ701 to B_SQ721 costs 31–33 ms with identical renderer, material and instance counts. The 21 chord cues add nothing.

All the cost arrives in one step at B_VQ701.1: +5 instances and +37,000 particles. The inventory at the end of the block names what is live:

CommunionWings_Unlit                        4 instances   35,072 particles
CommunionBodyAudioReactive                  7 instances    7,756 particles
260521CircleAudioReactiveCommunionRipples   3 instances    3,087 particles

The wings are 58% of every particle in the block, spawned once and held for 23 cues. Reducing them improves several minutes of stage time, not one moment. Nothing accumulates chord by chord — that theory is dead.

2. Cue spacing through the endings is a 10× lever, for free

Firing the heavy run (B_VQ801 → B_SQ901 → B_VQ901) and then probing the next cue at increasing gaps:

GapSettledWorstVFX

Identical content — 29 instances at every gap. It is a cliff, not a curve: everything expensive lives in the first few seconds and is gone by fifteen. Nothing leaks. If content were being held, 90 s would still be costly; it is not.

⚠️

B_VQ503.11 (“SHADOW ATTACK 1”) follows B_VQ901 (“Crown lowers for performer”) with five more cues between them. An attack beat after a reveal tends to be played tight — which lands it in the 5-second window. That single piece of timing may account for the worst frames in the piece.

The real spacing in performance is not recorded anywhere. Run the cue server during a rehearsal and it timestamps every fan-out; that would settle it.

This also re-explains B_VQ503.11, previously reported here as regressing 2.7×. It is a cheap cue — 29 ms in isolation — standing in the wake of the three most expensive ones. What got worse was the residue in front of it, not its own content.

3. What does not help

Two sweeps with baselines interleaved between every condition, on B_VQ801 and B_VQ503.11:

ToggleB_VQ801B_VQ503.11Verdict
voxels OFFno effect−7% (noise)not a factor
voxel fraction 0%no effect−9% (noise)not fill or geometry
VFX instances 29→23−26%−21%the only lever

Drawing 0% of the baked voxels costs the same as drawing all of them — so this is fixed per-draw overhead, not fill rate. And the second term of “instances × output contexts” is not available to attack: almost every graph has exactly one output context. Cost is instances, and consolidation is the whole job.

Post-processing is also ruled out: it defaults to off (StartMode = 1), every crash log confirms Post FX: OFF, and no post effects were added anywhere between the pre-optimisation baseline and v1.14.0.

Performance · Stability

The Beam Pro does not survive a sustained run

During testing on 21 August the show crashed four times on the Beam Pro, always under sustained load, always with the same signature:

Fatal signal 11 (SIGSEGV), fault addr 0x0 in tid (UnityMain)
  #00-#07  libunity.so
  #08      art_jni_trampoline
  #09      com.unity3d.player.z0.handleMessage

The whole stack sits inside libunity.so. There are no libil2cpp.so frames, so it is not the show’s C# code, and no XREAL frames, so it is not the glasses SDK. It is a null dereference inside Unity’s own engine.

⚠️

Correction, 23 August: the load theory does not hold. This page previously said the crash was triggered by resource pressure and that reducing load was the practical fix. Three crashes measured on 23 August contradict that. They landed at B_SQ719 and twice around B_VQ701, and in every case the device was comfortable — 35 ms frame times, and one occurred during the fastest trial of its set. Do not optimise against this theory.

What the three crashes do share is location: all of them fall inside the SQ7xx “How Long” chord sequence, matching the earlier cluster at cues 21, 22, 25, 26, 32 and 34. That, not load, is where to look.

It is also intermittent: across valid passes on 23 August there were 2 native crashes in roughly 6 clean traversals of the same sequence, so a single clean run proves nothing.

The tablet ran the same cues throughout and did not crash.

The stack, captured 24 August

A crash was finally caught with both logcat buffers streaming to disk. /data/tombstones is Permission denied on this unrooted device and the app is not debuggable, but the crash buffer carries the full backtrace and needs no permissions.

signal 11 (SIGSEGV), code 1 (SEGV_MAPERR), fault addr 0x0
tid (UnityMain)

#00-#07  libunity.so            (BuildId b5d0433965df40ad)
#08      art_jni_trampoline
#09      com.unity3d.player.z0.handleMessage +456
#10-#12  android.os.Handler.dispatchMessage -> Looper.loop
#13      com.unity3d.player.B0.run

A null dereference — fault addr 0x0 with SEGV_MAPERR means reading through a null, not a corrupted or dangling pointer. Eight frames inside libunity.so and no libil2cpp.so frames at all, so it is not the show’s C# code. Entered from the Java message loop via JNI — Unity’s own frame pump. The last managed activity was VisualCueScheduler:RaiseCueReady, about half a second earlier.

The offsets plus the BuildId and editor 6000.0.26f1 are what Unity would need to symbolicate. No symbols build is required to report it.

Reproduction rate, and where it is not

Roughly 1 crash in 3 full passes. But 88 traversals of the 7xx block alone produced none, so the chord material is not faulty by itself — it needs the state a full pass leaves behind. That also explains the intermittency, and why it does not track frame time.

⚠️

The connection is the larger risk. On 23–24 August the glasses disconnected ten times against two real crashes, cost five test runs and one phone restart. A fault that fires once in three passes loses a run occasionally; a link that drops every twenty minutes will end a performance. Check the cable and the Beam Pro’s USB-C port before optimising anything.

Memory is not the cause. Across 300 cues and six passes the pass-start figure drifted by 1.1 MB and the native heap held at 84.7 MB. There is no leak.

Performance · Method

How to reproduce this

  1. Install the build and launch it from the launcher on the device, not via adb shell am start — the direct activity start skips XR initialisation and renders mono. An hour of measurement was lost to this before the build began reporting its XR state.
  2. Confirm XR-STATE presenting=GLASSES in a fresh log window. Anything else and the figures are not stereo.
  3. Set the screen timeout long: adb shell settings put system screen_off_timeout 3600000. The default 60 seconds suspends the app mid-run, and the XREAL plugin then quits it.
  4. Fire cues at 15 second intervals. Under about 4 seconds measures the spawn rather than the settled scene.
  5. Read cost <cue>: <median> -> <settled> ms (worst <worst>, …) from the log.
⚠️

Warm up before measuring anything. A cue fired on a freshly launched app measures a different show. B_VQ503.11 reports 2 VFX instances and 661 particles cold against 29 and 37,346 after the sequence has run — 16.7 ms against 85 ms. Many earlier cues are {ENABLE} cues that add figures and never remove them, so the scene the show renders only exists after the cues before it have fired. This invalidates any single-cue measurement taken cold, including parts of the v1.7.0 table below.

⚠️

Measure a baseline between every condition, not just at the ends. One sweep opened at 186 ms and closed at 24 ms on the same measurement — warm-up transients decaying through the run. Everything between two baselines that far apart is worthless. Interleaved baselines make each toggle comparable to the one nearest it in time.

⚠️

Ask Android why a process ended. dumpsys activity exit-info distinguishes reason=5 APP CRASH (NATIVE) status=11 (a real SIGSEGV) from reason=2 SIGNALED status=9 (SIGKILL on glasses disconnect). Inferring it from logs produced three wrong calls in one day, including two “crashes” that were adb timeouts against a process that never died.

⚠️

The median alone is useless here. The display caps at 30 fps, so a cue coasting and a cue barely coping both read 33.4 ms. The worst frame is what separates headroom from none, which is why it was added to the log.
